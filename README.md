# An analysis of the development in Danish marriage patterns since 1971
Authors: Kathrine Andersen, Emma Maria Kristensen Larsen

# Project Description
This project investigates how women's economic and social emanicipation has changed the Danish marriage patterns from 1971 to 2025, using RStudio to wrangling and analysing data from Denmark's Statistics and KVINFO. 

The aim of our project is to identiofy the development in Danish marriage patterns from 1971 to 2025 and relate it to the third wave of feminism in Denmark, where women entered the labor force, gained sexual liberation and gradually changed views on gender roles. 

The analysis focuses on civil status, age of marriage, women's participation in labor force and women's age at childbirth. It examines the developments in these categories and compare it to social, cultural, political and economic factors in society during this period of time. 

The project was developed as part of a digital portfolio/final assignment.


# Results
The main findings of the project show that there was a significant change in marriage patterns since the 1970s due to social, political and economic circumstances. Fewer women aged 18-35 gets married than just fifty-five years ago and the average age of marriage has increased by several years. We conclude that women's participation in labor force, sexual liberation and social norms have influenced these marital developments.
